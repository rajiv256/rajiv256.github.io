# Eurocentrism in everyday life

I was listening to the audiobook “The Light Eaters” by the journalist
Zoe Schlanger, where she went on a tangent discussing how the perception
of plants as inanimate has its roots (no pun intended) in the
Eurocentric view that humans are the superior beings and are preferred
by God as they were in the image of God. This is a view challenged by
many indigenous cultures, which treat plants as a part of their family.
For instance, we use the term \`vegetable’ to refer to a physically and
mentally stunted person. This got me thinking. What other aspects of our
life are biased by such Eurocentric views. I can certainly read a book
about it and obtain a list. But I think it’d be better if I document
them based on my own experiences in life.

First, and a slightly funny example I know is from my favorite TV show
\`The West Wing’. In one of the episodes with the “Big Block of Cheese
Day” (every wingnut knows about this), the anthropologists from
Cartographers for Social Equality reveals how we have been conditioned
to think of the world as a flat map, with Europe at the center. This is
a Eurocentric view that has been ingrained in our education system and
media for centuries. In fact , the Mercator projection, which is
commonly used in maps, distorts the size and shape of countries, making
Europe appear larger than it actually is. Alternatively, the Peter’s
projection, which is less frequently used, presents a more accurate
representation of the world.

Another one I got to know is that calling the Arab countries, “Middle
East” is a Eurocentric view. The term was coined by the British in the
19th century to describe the region between Europe and the Far East.
This term is still widely used today, despite the fact that it is
geographically inaccurate and reinforces a Eurocentric perspective.
Better way to refer to the region would be “West Asia” or “Southwest
Asia”.
